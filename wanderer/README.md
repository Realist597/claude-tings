# The Wanderer (R6)

A ranger outfit based on your reference, with a cowboy hat and goggles added. It's restyled black, with deep purple and
silver accents, and fitted to the same R6 body as the Dark Knight armour (`../armor/r6_rig_body.fbx`).

- **Hat:**
  - black felt with a cattleman crease, and a brim curled up at the sides
  - purple band with silver studs and a silver concho
  - black feather fading to purple at the tip
  - goggles pushed up onto the crown: black-purple metal cups, silver bezels, domed purple lenses, and a strap
    buckled at the back
- **Torso:**
  - a tall stand-up collar, turned up over the mouth, lined and folded over in purple
  - a torn short capelet over the chest and shoulders
  - a black shirt
  - two crossed belts with silver buckles
- **Cape:**
  - a long, torn cape, longest on the left
  - lined in deep purple
- **Scarf:** a purple scarf tail streaming from the collar, ending in a swallowtail
- **Arms:**
  - the capelet's drape over each shoulder
  - black-purple bracers with silver rims, spikes and buckled straps
  - fingerless gloves
  - on the left shoulder only, a leather pauldron with two lames, edged and riveted in silver
- **Legs:**
  - trousers bloused over the boots, with a riveted seam
  - kite-shaped knee guards with silver diamonds
  - tall boots with crossing straps and a buckle
  - on the right leg only, two thigh straps and a pouch hung from the slung belt

The cloth has real folds:

- folds fan out down the capelet and the cape
- the shirt bunches over the belts and the sleeves bunch above the bracers
- the trousers blouse over the boots and crease behind the knees
- the collar crumples

These big folds are geometry, shaded smooth. The fine creases are baked into the normal maps, so they cost no
triangles. Every surface is a clean quad grid. Hard edges, like the buckles, spikes and torn hems, stay sharp.

## What's in `export/`

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `WandererOutfit.fbx` | Eight meshes: `Wanderer_Hat`, `Wanderer_Torso`, `Wanderer_LeftArm`, `Wanderer_RightArm`, `Wanderer_LeftLeg`, `Wanderer_RightLeg`, and the cloth `Wanderer_Cape` and `Wanderer_Scarf`, which have bones. Also their two armatures, and six `Ref_*` parts: the R6 body it was fitted to |
| `Wanderer_<Part>_Color.png` | Base colour (sRGB) |
| `Wanderer_<Part>_Normal.png` | Tangent-space normal map, OpenGL (+Y), as Roblox expects |
| `Wanderer_<Part>_Roughness.png` | Roughness |
| `Wanderer_<Part>_Metalness.png` | Metalness |
| `preview.png`, `preview_closeup.png`, `preview_side.png` | Renders using the baked maps, so they show what Roblox will show |

Every mesh stays under Roblox's 20,000-triangle limit, and every texture is 1024 × 1024.

## Putting it in your game

1. **Import:** in Studio, go to Avatar → Import 3D (or File → Import 3D) and pick `WandererOutfit.fbx`. Import it as
   one model.
2. **Textures:** select each `Wanderer_*` MeshPart and add a **SurfaceAppearance** if the importer didn't make one.
   Set its `ColorMap`, `NormalMap`, `RoughnessMap` and `MetalnessMap` to that part's four PNGs.
3. **Store it:** rename the model to `WandererOutfit` and move it into **ServerStorage**.
4. **Script:** put `WandererOutfit.server.lua` in **ServerScriptService** as a Script.

Players then spawn wearing it. Each piece is welded to its body part (the hat to the head, the cape and scarf to the
torso), so it moves with R6 animations. Other hats and hair are removed so they don't poke through the cowboy hat;
turn that off with `HIDE_ACCESSORIES`. If the outfit comes in facing backwards, set `FLIP = true`.

## The cloth bones

The cape and the scarf are rigged for you to animate, for example with Smart Bones:

| Mesh | Root bone | Chains |
|---|---|---|
| `Wanderer_Cape` | `CapeRoot` | three chains of four, `Cape_<chain>_<0..3>`, top to bottom |
| `Wanderer_Scarf` | `ScarfRoot` (inside the collar) | one chain of five along the scarf, `Scarf_0..4`, root to tip |

Each root should stay still. Every vertex has at most four bone influences, and the weights blend between neighbouring
bones so the cloth bends smoothly.

## Rebuilding

Everything is generated by `build_wanderer.py`, which borrows the armour script's plate, bake and rig tools. Run it
with the pip `bpy` module or Blender 4.2+:

```
python build_wanderer.py -- --stage preview   # quick look with live materials
python build_wanderer.py -- --stage stats     # triangle counts per piece and per mesh
python build_wanderer.py -- --stage all       # bake maps, render previews, export the FBX
```

Colours are set in `materials()`. Each piece's shape is in `hat()`, `collar()`, `shirt_and_belts()`, `capelet()`,
`cape()`, `scarf()`, `arm()`, `pauldron()`, `leg()` and `holster()`. The torn hems come from `tatter()`, which is
seeded, so they rebuild identically.
